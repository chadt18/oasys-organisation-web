Read the two-minute overview
Councils already have specialist software. But the work between those systems can still take time: checking one record against another, following up exceptions, finding the current rule and working out who has authority to act. OASYS Organisation is exploring an operating layer built around a person's actual role. Rather than replacing the council's systems or creating another full database, it could draw approved, minimum-necessary information into one work view. Imagine a planning officer opening their day. Instead of searching across different screens, they see which submissions need checking, why each one has been flagged, when the source information was updated and where to find the original evidence. The officer reviews the information and decides what happens next. This is not simply another dashboard. The proposed difference is the combination of task-specific evidence, transparent decision ownership, tightly bounded permissions and a clear handback to the systems that already hold the official record. If existing software does the job well, there may be no reason to add OASYS. Approved guidance and realistic practice could also sit alongside the work, helping organisations preserve knowledge and develop employees without replacing supervisors or human collaboration. Learning is an optional capability, not a requirement for the operating software. The concept is at discovery stage. We're looking for public-service teams with one recurring workflow that could be simpler, clearer or more reliable. The first step is to understand the problem and check whether this approach genuinely adds value.

Who we want to hear from

Public-service teams, managers and digital leaders.

We are interested in local-authority teams, NHS non-clinical service leads and other public organisations. The starting point is a real role, a recurring hand-off or exception and the experience of the people doing the work. We would first check what existing software can already solve.

Available at this stage: an explanatory concept, fictional examples and a discovery conversation. No live council demonstration, integrations, commercial pricing or procurement listing is offered.

The operational gap

Often, the issue is the work between systems.

Local authorities already run specialist case, finance, planning and environmental-service software. The challenge may be the hand-offs: verifying a discrepancy, locating current guidance, identifying a decision owner or helping a new colleague understand the process.

Existing systems→Approved information→Clear role-specific work→Human decision

If the organisation's existing system already solves a problem well, OASYS should not duplicate it.

The OASYS approach

Start with the professional, not the software.

01 / Human first

Respect the expertise behind the work.

Build the working view around the professional's actual role and judgement, not a wall of generic metrics. Technology prepares and supports; the authorised person decides.

02 / Evidence in context

Make the next step understandable.

Show the source, freshness, relevant rule and uncertainty together, so people can verify information rather than simply trust an AI suggestion.

03 / Organisation in control

Preserve authority and independence.

Role permissions, approval, audit, manual fallback and a practical exit are design requirements. The public body retains control of its own systems and data.

Working and learning

Make the work clearer. Help the knowledge endure.

Role guidance could sit alongside the work, helping employees understand the purpose behind approved procedures. Synthetic exercises could support supervised practice. The same digital-workplace skills could later form a separate training offer without requiring anyone to use the operational software.

Optional learning beside work

Approved procedures, explanations and fictional examples aligned to the role. Training never silently changes an employee's permissions.

Independent digital-readiness training

A possible standalone programme covering trustworthy data, digital workflows, dashboards, privacy and sensible use of AI.

Trust and service continuity

Designed to work with existing authority — not override it.

Minimum necessary data

Explore task-specific access, client separation and controlled pseudonymisation. Pseudonymised records remain subject to data-protection law.

Human authority

Important service decisions remain with authorised people. Any future automated actions would be bounded, justified and reviewed.

Customer control and exit

The source system remains authoritative. Organisations should be able to understand the workflow, maintain service continuity and leave OASYS.

Important: the concept is being designed with UK public-sector standards and ICO guidance in mind. No specific council integration, official approval, certification or production compliance claim is being made.
