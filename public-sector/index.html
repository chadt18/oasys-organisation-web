Read the two-minute overview
Think about a council officer starting work. They're good at their job, but the information they need is spread across different systems. Before they can act, they may have to check several screens, find the latest rule and work out which record is right. OASYS is an idea for making that part of the job easier. It would sit alongside the software a council or other public organisation already uses. It could bring together the information a member of staff is allowed to see, show what needs checking and point back to where each detail came from. Imagine a planning team with fifty consultation responses. One is missing information. Another might be a duplicate. Instead of searching through everything, the officer could see those two items, open the original responses and decide what to do. OASYS wouldn't make the planning decision. That's the important difference we're exploring. Rather than another dashboard full of numbers, it would help people understand what needs their attention, why it matters and what they are allowed to do next. The organisation would still own its records and stay in charge. Clear instructions and practice examples could sit alongside the work too, if a team found them useful. But training isn't the main product. We haven't built a live council or NHS service. We're looking for teams who can tell us about one part of the job that takes more effort than it should. Together, we can see whether OASYS would genuinely help.

The operational gap

The tricky part is often what happens between systems.

Different teams use different software. The hard part can be checking whether records agree, finding the right instructions, knowing who needs to act or helping someone new understand the process.

Existing systems→Information your team is allowed to use→The work that needs attention→Human decision

If your current software already does the job well, we shouldn't add another screen.

The OASYS approach

Start with the person doing the work.

01 / Human first

Good judgement belongs to people.

Show each person what matters to their job, not another wall of charts. OASYS could help prepare the information. The person responsible makes the decision.

02 / Evidence in context

Show where the information came from.

Let people check the original record, when it was updated and why something has been flagged. If the answer isn't clear, say so.

03 / Organisation in control

Your organisation stays in charge.

Your team decides who can see or change things. Important actions need the right approval. Your existing systems keep the official records, and you should be able to stop using OASYS without losing control.

OASYS Levels 1–5

The same five levels, with public-service safeguards.

The levels describe what OASYS could be allowed to do, not five packages to buy. The higher the level, the more important the permissions, checks and human oversight. These Public Sector examples are design proposals, not deployed features.

People review every decision→Carefully limited routine actions
Level 1 · Brief

See what needs attention

A planning officer sees 50 responses, two possible exceptions and links to their original records. Nothing is changed automatically.

Human control: the officer checks the evidence and decides what to do.

Level 2 · Recommend

See possible next steps

Show approved ways to check an incomplete record or a possible duplicate, with the relevant council guidance.

Human control: staff choose the next step, not the software.

Level 3 · Draft

Prepare work for review

Prepare a draft case note or request for missing information using the council's approved wording.

Human control: a person reviews and releases every draft.

Level 4 · Approval-gated action

Act only after approval

A permitted routine update could be prepared, but nothing is sent or changed until an authorised person approves it.

Human control: roles, approval and an audit record are required.

Level 5 · Bounded automation

Automate only safe, agreed tasks

In a future approved service, tightly defined, reversible, low-risk housekeeping could run within set limits.

Human control: staff set the limits, review outcomes and can stop it immediately. No automatic planning, welfare or clinical decisions.

Trust and service continuity

People stay responsible. Records stay where they belong.

The more connected a public-service workflow becomes, the clearer its responsibilities need to be. These are checks we'd design and test with each organisation, not guarantees of a finished product.

Only the right information

Agree which source systems and fields a team actually needs. Access would be enforced by role and purpose, not simply hidden on a screen. Different organisations' information must stay separate.

People approve important decisions

Show the record, the rule, any uncertainty and the person responsible. OASYS might prepare a note, but official decisions remain with authorised staff. Any future action would need a traceable approval.

Keep a route back

Official records stay in the original system. Staff should be able to find the source, correct an error, keep working manually, review what happened and leave OASYS without losing the service.

What would we check before connecting real systems?
  • Source and ownership: who owns the record, where does it live and which version is approved?
  • Permission and privacy: why is access needed, who approves it and what information can be used?
  • Human review: who can act, what happens if the information is wrong and how is the decision recorded?
  • Continuity: how does the team work if a source or OASYS is unavailable, and how do they exit?

NHS work would also need its own information-governance, security and, where relevant, clinical-safety assessment.

Who could support or administer a connected system?

Before any real deployment, the public body and OASYS would document authorised users, supplier engineers, processing locations, subcontractors, approval for privileged access, access expiry and independent monitoring. Having technical access does not grant permission to use a person's information for another purpose. These controls are proposed requirements, not tested OASYS capabilities.

How could we challenge an error or leave OASYS?

Staff should be able to see the original source, challenge a suggestion and record who corrected it. A buyer would also need to test an export, safe handover, withdrawal of supplier permissions, agreed deletion rules and a way to keep the service operating without OASYS. No live migration or exit test has been completed.

Current status: these are design requirements guided by UK public-sector and data-protection standards. OASYS does not claim government approval, certification or live council or NHS connections.
