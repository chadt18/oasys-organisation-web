A short explanation

OASYS Public Sector in two minutes

A quick explanation of what OASYS could help with, and why people remain in charge.

Read the audio transcript
Think about a council officer starting work. They're good at their job, but the information they need is spread across different systems. Before they can act, they may have to check several screens, find the latest rule and work out which record is right. OASYS is an idea for making that part of the job easier. It would sit alongside the software a council or other public organisation already uses. It could bring together the information a member of staff is allowed to see, show what needs checking and point back to where each detail came from. Imagine a planning team with fifty consultation responses. One is missing information. Another might be a duplicate. Instead of searching through everything, the officer could see those two items, open the original responses and decide what to do. OASYS wouldn't make the planning decision. That's the important difference we're exploring. Rather than another dashboard full of numbers, it would help people understand what needs their attention, why it matters and what they are allowed to do next. The organisation would still own its records and stay in charge. Clear instructions and practice examples could sit alongside the work too, if a team found them useful. But training isn't the main product. We haven't built a live council or NHS service. We're looking for teams who can tell us about one part of the job that takes more effort than it should. Together, we can see whether OASYS would genuinely help.

Who we want to hear from

Public-service teams, managers and digital leaders.

We're keen to hear from council teams, NHS staff working in non-clinical services and other public organisations. Tell us about a job that takes more effort than it should. We'll start with what your team already does and what its software can already handle.

What you can explore today: the idea, made-up examples and a conversation about your team's work. OASYS is not yet available as a council or NHS system.

The operational gap

The tricky part is often what happens between systems.

Different teams use different software. The hard part can be checking whether records agree, finding the right instructions, knowing who needs to act or helping someone new understand the process.

Existing systems→Information your team is allowed to use→The work that needs attention→Human decision

If your current software already does the job well, we shouldn't add another screen.

The OASYS approach

Start with the person doing the work.

01 / Human first

Good judgement belongs to people.

Show each person what matters to their job, not another wall of charts. OASYS could help prepare the information. The person responsible makes the decision.

02 / Evidence in context

Show where the information came from.

Let people check the original record, when it was updated and why something has been flagged. If the answer isn't clear, say so.

03 / Organisation in control

Your organisation stays in charge.

Your team decides who can see or change things. Important actions need the right approval. Your existing systems keep the official records, and you should be able to stop using OASYS without losing control.

Illustrative applications

Where could this make a difference?

Councils and the NHS work in different ways. These are just examples to explore, not systems we've connected.

Illustrative Scenario A · Planning administration

Checking consultation responses

Bring missing details and possible duplicate responses to an officer's attention, with the original responses easy to check. The officer stays responsible.

Illustrative Scenario B · Environmental services

Looking into missed bin collections

Help staff spot where a report and the collection record don't agree, while keeping the council's waste system in place.

Illustrative Scenario C · Procurement teams

Keeping track of contracts

Bring deadlines, missing paperwork and who needs to approve what into one clear view.

Illustrative Scenario D · Future / higher sensitivity

Checking supporting documents

A possible later use: help an authorised officer spot missing documents. This would need much stricter checks before any real use.

Illustrative Scenario E · NHS non-clinical operations

Handing over hospital maintenance jobs

Show a fictional NHS estates team which maintenance jobs need checking, what contractors have reported and who needs to approve the next step. No patient details or clinical decisions.

These are ideas to discuss, not services already in use. Any NHS work would need separate privacy, security and safety checks.

Working and learning

Keep useful know-how close to the work.

Approved instructions could sit beside a task, so someone can check how and why it's done. Staff could practise with made-up examples. General digital-skills training could also be offered separately.

Help when you need it

Clear instructions and practice examples for a particular job. Learning something new would never automatically give someone more access.

Digital skills as a separate course

A possible course on using digital systems, checking information, protecting privacy and working sensibly with AI.

Trust and service continuity

Designed to work with existing authority — not override it.

Only what's needed

Give people access only to the information needed for their job. Keep different organisations' data separate. Privacy law still applies.

People make important decisions

Software can point something out or prepare a task. The right person still makes important decisions. Any future automated steps would need strict limits and checks.

You can still work without us

Your existing system keeps the official information. Your team should be able to carry on working even if OASYS is switched off.

Important: we're using UK public-sector and data-protection guidance while developing this idea. We are not claiming government approval, certification or live council or NHS connections.
