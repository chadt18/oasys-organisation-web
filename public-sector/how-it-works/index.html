Illustrative Scenario A · Fictional planning administration

Planning consultations: one controlled process, not fifty scattered screens.

Imagine a planning administration team receiving 50 fictional consultation submissions. A record is incomplete, a possible duplicate needs checking and one piece of text may be difficult to classify. OASYS could help the officer see those exceptions, while preserving every original submission for review.

Receive approved information

Import only permitted records and record source, date and completeness. Start any prototype with fictional information.

Validate without inventing answers

Defined checks flag missing fields, potential duplicates and uncertain material. The original submissions remain available.

Show what deserves attention

The officer sees a short exception queue with the evidence, rule and reason for the flag.

Keep the officer in charge

An authorised employee verifies the material, resolves conflicts and follows the council's actual planning procedures.

Support learning where helpful

An optional fictional practice exercise teaches a trainee how to challenge a misleading summary and locate the approved rule.

Keep the work traceable

Record who reviewed the issue, what the original source showed and what action was authorised. The planning system remains authoritative.

Illustrative process only. There is no live planning-system connection or interactive demo on this page yet.

Two independent capabilities

Useful work support should not depend on buying training.

Operational workspace

Help me do the job correctly today.

An authorised view of active tasks, exceptions, sources and responsibilities. Training is not required for every user or every role.

Optional learning

Help me understand the job and grow my skills.

Approved task explanations and synthetic practice can be embedded, while a future standalone programme could teach general digital-work skills separately.

Important: a job description might help identify training needs, but it cannot automatically become an approved operating procedure. An authorised service owner must validate policies and competency expectations.

Questions a public authority should ask

Designed for accountable people and services.

Would OASYS replace our current council or NHS systems?

No. The concept starts with the systems and teams already in place. We would first test whether their existing workflows, including NHS Federated Data Platform tools where relevant, already solve the issue. If so, OASYS should not add another layer.

Could the NHS use OASYS?

Potentially for a narrowly defined non-clinical operational workflow, such as a fictional estates-maintenance handover, but this is only a discovery possibility. OASYS has no NHS systems connected and does not currently process patient data. An NHS deployment would require organisation-specific information governance, security and procurement review; health IT and clinical workflows may also trigger NHS clinical-safety and DTAC requirements. Professional and clinical decisions remain with authorised staff.

Would it store sensitive citizen records?

That cannot be answered with a universal claim. A specific workflow would require a documented purpose, minimum necessary fields, controller/processor responsibilities, lawful authority, retention and security design. Initial demonstrations would use synthetic data.

Could an AI automatically change decisions or accounts?

No autonomous decision or live-system modification is part of the proposed first demonstration. Any future actions require a tightly defined permission, applicable lawful basis, service-owner approval and testing; high-impact decisions remain accountable to authorised people.

Is OASYS government approved or ICO certified?

No such approval or certification is claimed. The architecture is being developed with data protection, accessibility, security, interoperability and service continuity in mind; suitability for each actual deployment would need evidence and formal review.

What happens if we stop using OASYS?

The design objective is for existing source systems to remain usable, with documented data export, access revocation, approved retention, migration and manual fallback. These are requirements to prove, not existing contractual guarantees.

Can we see a working demonstration now?

A separate interactive demonstration is planned for a later phase. This page currently explains a fictional workflow and the proposed controls; it does not connect to an actual council system.
