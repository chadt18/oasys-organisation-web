Illustrative Scenario A · Fictional planning administration

Checking planning responses without hunting through screens.

Imagine a planning team receives 50 responses to a consultation. One is missing information and another looks like it might be a duplicate. OASYS could point out those two items and let the officer check each original response.

Bring in what the team needs

Use only information the team is allowed to see, with its source and date clearly shown.

Spot what needs checking

Flag missing details and possible duplicates without guessing what the answer should be.

Show the reason for each flag

The officer can see what needs a look and open the original record.

Let the officer decide

The officer uses their experience and the council's procedures to decide what happens.

Offer guidance if it's useful

A new starter could practise with a made-up example and learn when to check the original instructions.

Keep a clear record

Keep track of who checked what and what they decided. The council's planning system still holds the official record.

Questions a public authority should ask

A few important questions, answered plainly.

Does OASYS replace the software we already use?

No. We'd start with the software you already have, including existing NHS tools where relevant. If it does the job well, there's no reason to add OASYS.

Could this be useful in the NHS?

Possibly for everyday work outside clinical care, such as handing over hospital maintenance jobs. We have no NHS connections and don't handle patient information. Any real use would need NHS privacy, security and purchasing checks. Clinical work would need additional safety checks and appropriate expert approval.

Would it copy private information into another database?

The aim is to use as little information as possible and keep official records in existing systems. We would agree exactly what information is needed, who can use it and how it's protected before any real connection. Early examples use made-up data.

Could AI make decisions for staff?

Not in this first concept. OASYS might highlight a problem or prepare information, but important decisions stay with authorised people. Any future automated steps would need clear limits and approval.

Has the public-sector version been officially approved?

No. We're designing with public-sector requirements in mind, but this version has not been certified, approved or deployed for a council or NHS organisation.

What if we decide to stop using it?

Your original systems should still work. A proper product would need a clear way to switch it off, take back any information and carry on working. That's a design goal we would test, not a promise we've already proved.

Can we try a demo?

Not yet. This page shows a made-up planning example, not a connected product. We may build a clickable demo after speaking with interested teams.
